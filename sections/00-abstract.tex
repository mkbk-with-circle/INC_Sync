Some expert-parallel communication paths use a pre-barrier before payload injection and a post-barrier after sender-side transfer work. At small batches, these stages can occupy a substantial fraction of the operator. We study cross-node DeepEP V2 Direct Dispatch and Combine on two H200 nodes. At eight tokens per rank and EP8, the pre-barrier occupies about 20\% of the traced operator. In Qwen3-30B-A3B decode, the pre-barrier accounts for 15.2--16.7\% of the sampled cross-node GPU step, compared with 7.9--9.3\% within one node.

We propose \sys{}, a design that coordinates these transfer boundaries in the network. A rank announces readiness and uploads directly into network staging; the network releases data after the required readiness conditions hold. At completion, a sealed traffic plan lets the network append an ordered notification after a destination's final data unit. We specify the visibility, buffer-lifetime, and progress requirements for these operations and analyze their effect on the communication critical path. Under a symmetric reference schedule with an exposed control traversal at each boundary, the opportunity approaches one fabric RTT before added overhead. The measurements characterize the baseline; the design's net benefit remains to be evaluated on an implementation.
