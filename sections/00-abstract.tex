Expert-parallel (EP) communication in Mixture-of-Experts inference coordinates its
participants twice per collective: once before payload injection, to establish that
peers are ready to receive, and once after it, to establish that a destination's
input is complete. Both exchanges sit on the critical path, and neither shrinks with
the payload. At the batch sizes decode actually runs at, they stop being rounding
error. On two H200 nodes running DeepEP V2's cross-node Direct path at eight tokens
per rank, the entry exchange occupies 19.8--20.6\% of the traced operator and the
completion-signal window a further 17.7--19.3\%. Sweeping the batch sixteen-fold leaves
both windows flat in absolute terms, at 14.8--15.4\us{} and 11.2--14.3\us{}, while
their shares fall monotonically below 5\% of a now much longer operator. The same asymmetry appears in serving: under Qwen3-30B-A3B decode at fixed
EP8, entry synchronization consumes 15.2--16.7\% of a cross-node GPU step against
7.9--9.3\% within a single node.

We argue this cost is a placement artifact rather than an intrinsic one, and that the
fan-in point of the network is where it should be resolved. We present \sys{}, an
in-network coordination design with two mechanisms. At the head, a rank announces
readiness and uploads immediately into bounded network staging; the in-network node
aggregates readiness and releases buffered data once the synchronization domain is
ready, overlapping peer announcement with transfer instead of serializing it before
transfer. At the tail, the node seals a traffic plan for each destination and appends
an ordered notification behind that destination's final data unit, so the destination
learns its input is complete without waiting for a sender to return through its own
completion path and originate a fresh exchange. Adapting Swift's
endpoint--fabric delay decomposition, we separate the existence of a synchronization
stage from its exposed latency, and show that under a symmetric reference each
eligible boundary yields approximately one one-way traversal, approaching one fabric
RTT across both before added overhead. We state the visibility, buffer-ownership,
counting, and admission conditions the design requires, and identify which baseline
paths satisfy them. This is a design and measurement study: the baselines are
measured, the latency opportunity is analytical, and no in-network prototype is
evaluated.
