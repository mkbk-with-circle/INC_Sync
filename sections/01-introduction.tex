\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Mixture-of-experts (MoE) inference routes token activations to selected experts and combines their outputs. Expert parallelism distributes those experts across GPU ranks, requiring Dispatch and Combine communication at every MoE layer. Communication libraries use GPU-initiated transfers, preallocated buffers, and topology-aware paths to move these data efficiently~\cite{deepep,ncclep}.

Small batches make the coordination before transmission particularly important. A pre-barrier may prevent a rank from sending locally available tokens until it has received readiness signals from its peers. The token payload shrinks with batch size, but the readiness exchange still involves the same participating ranks.

Our measurements of DeepEP V2 Direct quantify this cost. With cross-node EP8, the pre-barrier lasts approximately 15\us{} across the measured small-batch configurations. It occupies about 20\% of Dispatch and Combine time at eight tokens per rank, falling to about 5\% at 128. In Qwen3-30B-A3B decode, the accumulated pre-barrier intervals account for 15.2--16.7\% of the sampled cross-node GPU step, compared with 7.9--9.3\% within one node.

The opportunity comes from separating upload from destination delivery. A rank may have data ready to send even while another rank is still preparing its receive buffer. If the network can hold early data, the first rank can begin uploading during this wait. The destination remains protected until its receive buffer is writable.

We present \sys{}, which places readiness aggregation and bounded data staging at an INC node. Ranks announce local readiness and upload without waiting for peer signals to return. INC records the ready participants, buffers early data, and starts forwarding when all required readiness conditions hold. The receiving ranks use preallocated symmetric buffers, so forwarding does not wait for a global receive-count calculation.

This paper contributes a characterization of pre-barrier costs in H200 microbenchmarks and MoE inference, an early-upload protocol with explicit buffer-reuse and backpressure rules, and an operation-level analysis of readiness/data overlap. Following Swift's separation of processing and network delay~\cite{swift}, the analysis identifies when early upload shortens the full communication operation. The benefit depends on rank readiness times, available staging, and the rate at which INC can forward buffered data.
