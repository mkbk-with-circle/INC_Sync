\section{Background and Motivation}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Expert-Parallel Communication}
\label{sec:epbackground}
An MoE layer selects up to $\topk$ experts for each token. Under expert parallelism, Dispatch sends activations to the ranks hosting those experts; Combine returns their outputs to the token owner~\cite{deepep,ncclep}. Routing determines the destinations and traffic volume. The exchange also carries metadata needed to place tokens into expert input buffers.

Ranks communicate readiness, counts, and routing information through collective operations or point-to-point writes and polling. A readiness signal indicates that the resources needed for the round are available. Communication buffers provide known landing positions; the final arrangement of tokens by expert may depend on exchanged counts.

EP implementations organize these steps differently. NCCL EP HT gathers routing metadata before Dispatch. DeepEP V2 Direct performs a pre-barrier, then processes counts alongside data. NCCL EP LL writes into reserved slots with fine-grained signals~\cite{ncclhtcode,deepepcode,ncclllcode}. These choices determine what must finish before a rank can send its first token.

\subsection{Pre-Barriers in DeepEP V2 Direct}
\label{sec:epsynchronization}
Direct Dispatch and Combine enter a pre-barrier before token transmission~\cite{deepepcode}. Each rank waits for readiness from the participating group and completes the required local synchronization. Dispatch subsequently exchanges counts and computes layout information alongside data movement; Combine uses routing established during Dispatch.

The Direct path preallocates communication buffers. This permits a distinction between moving data toward the destination and writing it into the destination buffer. An INC node can support that distinction within or across nodes when it covers the relevant traffic and preserves the required delivery order.

At fixed EP size, fewer tokens reduce data-transfer work while coordination still involves the same ranks. Our cross-node EP8 measurements show a pre-barrier duration near 15\us{}, occupying about 20\% of operator time at eight tokens per rank and 5\% at 128. Section~\ref{sec:evidence} reports the measurement method and results.

\subsection{Opportunities for In-Network Computing}
\label{sec:incopportunity}
In-network computing lets network devices process data while forwarding it, for example by summing values from several GPUs~\cite{sharp,switchml,dysharp}. Such devices can also retain temporary state across packets. For pre-barriers, the useful state is which ranks are ready, together with space to hold data that arrives before forwarding is allowed.

A locally ready rank can upload while INC collects the remaining readiness signals. INC holds early tokens until the required receive buffers are writable, then forwards both buffered and arriving data.

\paragraph{Key insight.}
A rank can upload locally ready data before all ranks are ready, provided INC buffers the data until the required readiness conditions hold. This overlaps readiness aggregation with the first part of data transfer.
