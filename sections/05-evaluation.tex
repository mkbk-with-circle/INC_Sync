\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evidence}
We ask three questions: how pre-barrier cost changes with token count, how it appears across EP configurations, and how much of an MoE serving step it occupies.

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Methodology}
\label{sec:setup}
Each node has eight H200 GPUs connected by NVSwitch and eight 400\,GbE RoCE interfaces. Cross-node runs use GIN's GDAKI backend, traffic class 162, PyTorch 2.13, CUDA 13, NCCL 2.31.2, and DeepEP V2 based on commit \texttt{01dc3aaa}~\cite{deepepcode}. Direct is selected with Hybrid disabled; available local communication within Direct is retained.

Microbenchmarks use $H=7168$, $\topk=8$, 256 experts, BF16, and balanced uniform routing. $T$ denotes tokens per rank. Each configuration has three process runs with 200 measured iterations. For each iteration, we report the shortest pre-barrier across ranks and normalize it by the same rank's trace-on operator time. Results are averaged over iterations and runs; error bars show variation across run means.

The EP8 token sweep and the EP-size comparison are separate campaigns. \autoref{fig:entry-cost} uses $T=8,32,64,128$ from one instrumented image. \autoref{tab:entry-constants} uses its listed points from the original campaign plus a later EP8/T64 batch; its constants are fitted only to those values.

\subsection{Effect of Token Count}
\label{sec:entry-eval}
\autoref{fig:entry-cost} reports pre-barrier duration and its share within a single Direct Dispatch or Combine operator at EP8. Dispatch and Combine remain near 15\us{} across the measured token counts. Their operator shares fall from 20.57\% and 19.84\% at $T=8$ to 4.88\% and 5.05\% at $T=128$. The nearly fixed pre-barrier is hardest to amortize when each rank sends only a few tokens.

\subsection{Pre-Barrier Cost across EP Sizes}
\label{sec:scale-eval}
Across the tested configurations, mean pre-barrier duration rises from approximately 11.6\us{} at EP4 to 18.3\us{} at EP16. Within each EP size, changing token count moves the mean by at most 0.16\us{}. \autoref{tab:entry-constants} summarizes both observations.

\subsection{Pre-Barrier Share in MoE Inference}
\label{sec:e2e}
We measure Qwen3-30B-A3B in BF16 with vLLM 0.27 and DeepEP V2 Direct. The model has $H=2048$, 128 experts, and $\topk=8$. Runs use actual routing, TP=1, EP8, and requests with 128 input and 128 output tokens. \autoref{fig:inference-share} reports one-node and cross-node sweeps from concurrency 1 to 256.

The serving build includes tracing and an expert-padding correctness fix. For decode, one active GPU step is sampled per rank and client window; each sampled step contains 48 Dispatch and 48 Combine calls. We sum the pre-barrier intervals from those calls, divide by the same rank's trace-on GPU-step time, and average over active rank-steps and three client windows on the same server instance. Thus \autoref{fig:inference-share} and \autoref{tab:serving-coverage} measure cumulative pre-barrier exposure within an MoE GPU step, whereas \autoref{fig:entry-cost} measures a single communication operator.

\input{figures/inference-share}

\paragraph{Request concurrency.}
Low-concurrency points activate only part of the EP group: C1 has one active rank, and the number of active ranks grows with concurrency. From C16 onward, all eight ranks are active. Over C16--C256, the one-node share falls from 14.91\% to 7.61\%, while the cross-node share falls from 18.73\% to 14.22\%. Pre-barriers therefore remain visible throughout decode, with greater exposure across nodes.

\paragraph{EP scale and workload.}
\autoref{tab:serving-coverage} holds decode traffic at 16 tokens per rank: pre-barriers occupy approximately 15\% of the GPU step at EP4, EP8, and EP16. Prefill processes 512 or 2048 tokens in its active step, reducing the share to 2.6--3.1\%. These serving workloads show how larger token batches amortize the repeated readiness cost.
