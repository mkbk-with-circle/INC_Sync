\section{Latency Analysis}
\label{sec:model}
We compare when the baseline and INC make the required input available for the next computation. The comparison includes rank readiness, data delivery, and completion notification.

\subsection{Delay Model}
\label{sec:delay-model}
Following Swift's separation of endpoint and network delay~\cite{swift}, we distinguish the time spent issuing a message, transporting it through the network, and observing it at the receiver:
\begin{equation}
 T_{\rm msg}=T_{\rm sender}+T_{\rm network}+T_{\rm receiver}.
 \label{eq:message}
\end{equation}
The network term includes propagation, serialization, and queuing. The endpoint terms include initiating the send and detecting the received signal. We use this decomposition to analyze EP synchronization, rather than adopting Swift's timestamping procedure.

A barrier additionally waits for participating ranks to become ready and for required local work to finish. Concurrent messages are awaited together, so the last required arrival determines completion. The measured barrier duration therefore cannot be treated as network propagation time or as the time INC necessarily saves.

\subsection{Overlapping the Pre-Barrier}
\label{sec:readiness-analysis}
Let $r_i$ be the time rank $i$ becomes locally ready, and $\mathcal{R}_i$ the peers it waits for. Let $u_{j,i}$ include the time to issue and deliver rank $j$'s READY to $i$, and $v_i$ the time for $i$ to observe the signals and finish its local synchronization. The baseline starts sending data at
\begin{equation}
 g_i=\max\left\{r_i,\max_{j\in\mathcal{R}_i}(r_j+u_{j,i})\right\}+v_i.
 \label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}

With INC, rank $i$ can start uploading at $r_i+\delta_i$, where $\delta_i$ is its READY and upload preparation time, provided PFC has not paused the link. Let $a_i$ be the time to send its complete READY/count-vector message to INC, and $\lambda_d$ the time destination $d$'s buffer becomes writable. INC can start forwarding to $d$ at
\begin{equation}
 o_d=\max\left\{\max_i(r_i+a_i),\lambda_d\right\}.
 \label{eq:release}
\end{equation}
Here $i$ ranges over the required participants. READY confirms buffer readiness; buffer addresses are fixed during initialization. INC does not wait for the count-vector sum before forwarding.

The difference is that data can already be traveling to INC while READY messages are being collected. Once forwarding starts, buffered tokens compete for the output link with newly arriving tokens. Output queuing, late ranks, and PFC pauses determine how much of the upload overlap reduces final delivery time. Figure~\ref{fig:timing} illustrates both unequal and simultaneous readiness.

\subsection{Overlapping the Post-Barrier}
\label{sec:directtail}
In Direct, ranks finish the required sender-side work before exchanging post-barrier signals~\cite{deepepcode,gdakicode}. Let $c_j$ be the time rank $j$ can issue its signal, $w_{j,d}$ the time to issue and deliver it to $d$, and $v_d$ the final observation time at $d$. The destination observes all required signals at
\begin{equation}
 z_d=\max_{j\in\mathcal{R}_d\cup\{d\}}(c_j+w_{j,d})+v_d.
 \label{eq:tailsignal}
\end{equation}
The set includes the destination's own local completion, with no network propagation for that term. Let $\tau_d$ be the time all required data is visible in its memory. The baseline communication is complete at $\max(\tau_d,z_d)$.

For INC, let $\tau'_d$ be the corresponding data-visibility time and $\eta_d$ the remaining delay until DONE is observed. This includes any unfinished count aggregation and the cost of delivering an ordered notification. The difference in communication completion times is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta T_{\rm tail,d}=\max(\tau_d,z_d)-(\tau'_d+\eta_d).
 \label{eq:tailgain}
\end{equation}
If data arrives at the same time in both designs, INC saves the post-barrier wait remaining after data visibility, less its notification delay. Changes in data delivery, forwarding across multiple paths, and notification processing must also be included. Any additional computation dependencies remain unchanged.

The measured post-barrier window in Section~\ref{sec:completion-eval} runs from signal initiation to signal observation. It does not locate $\tau_d$, so that entire window cannot be substituted for the saving above.

\paragraph{Ideal reference.}
\label{sec:ideal}
Consider simultaneous readiness, one-way network delay $L$, payload transmission duration $S$, and unchanged local processing time $P$. Suppose the baseline exposes one separate control traversal before data and another after data becomes visible:
\begin{equation}
 T_{\rm base}=P+L+(S+L)+L=P+S+3L.
 \label{eq:base}
\end{equation}
If INC receives READY before the following data, sustains the same forwarding rate, and finishes counting before notification is needed, upload can overlap the first traversal and DONE can accompany the final data delivery. With additional INC overhead $\epsilon$,
\begin{align}
 T_{\rm INC}&=P+S+L+\epsilon,\\
 \Delta T&=2L-\epsilon.
 \label{eq:gain}
\end{align}
Thus, the ideal opportunity is one network RTT before additional overhead. Unequal readiness, queuing, and backpressure must instead be evaluated using the actual data and notification times; measured pre-barrier and post-barrier durations are not directly additive savings.
