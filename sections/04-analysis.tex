\section{Latency Analysis}
\label{sec:model}
We describe a complete Dispatch or Combine operation as three stages: READY, data transfer, and post-barrier. The baseline executes them in sequence. INC allows upload to proceed during READY collection, followed by the post-barrier.

\subsection{Three-Stage Model}
\label{sec:delay-model}
Let $T_{\rm ready}$ be the pre-barrier duration, $T_{\rm data}$ the data-transfer phase, and $T_{\rm post}$ the post-barrier and subsequent local epilogue. The data phase includes the sender-side work needed to enter the post-barrier. Along the baseline operation's critical path,
\begin{equation}
 T_{\rm base}=T_{\rm ready}+T_{\rm data}+T_{\rm post}.
 \label{eq:base}
\end{equation}
These intervals describe one execution sequence. They are not assembled from independently selected per-rank measurements. Following Swift~\cite{swift}, each stage can contain both processing and network delay; in particular, $T_{\rm ready}$ includes signal handling and peer waiting as well as network transmission.

\subsection{Overlapping READY and Data Transfer}
\label{sec:readiness-analysis}
Figure~\ref{fig:timing} shows the change in execution order. In Direct, DATA starts after READY finishes. With INC, a rank starts uploading while INC collects readiness signals. Early data can travel to INC or wait in its buffers; writes to the destination begin only after the required ranks and receive buffers are ready.

For an ideal parallel schedule with the same stage workloads and no extra delay to forwarding, the operation takes
\begin{equation}
 T_{\rm INC}^{\rm ref}=
 \max(T_{\rm ready},T_{\rm data})+T_{\rm post}+T_{\rm extra},
 \label{eq:inc}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\rm extra}$ is additional INC processing time that remains on the critical path. When $T_{\rm ready}\leq T_{\rm data}$ and readiness collection fits within the data phase, the post-barrier follows the overlapping stages. In this full-overlap reference,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta T^{\rm ref}=T_{\rm ready}-T_{\rm extra}.
 \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
Thus, early upload can hide the READY stage rather than leaving it ahead of data transfer. Under simultaneous readiness and symmetric network paths, the hidden network component can be one one-way traversal, approximately half a network RTT. The pre-barrier's measured duration also contains processing and waiting time.

\subsection{When the Overlap Shortens the Operation}
\label{sec:skew}
The parallel reference requires enough staging and forwarding bandwidth for READY collection to fit within the data phase without extending it. If buffered data must wait longer at INC, that wait extends the actual data-transfer phase. Output queuing and PFC pauses can likewise reduce the overlap.

With unequal rank readiness, early ranks can upload while later ranks prepare, but forwarding still waits for all required READY signals. Operation latency is determined by the last rank to finish. The benefit therefore depends on whether the overlap advances that finish time, together with any change in the subsequent completion work.
